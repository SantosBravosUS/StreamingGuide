01 · STREAMING BASICS

UNDERSTANDING SPOTIFY STREAMS

A stream is a play of a track on Spotify that meets the platform's counting requirements. Spotify's published guidance states that a track needs to be played for at least 30 seconds to count as a stream.

THE 30-SECOND RULE
A track must be played for at least 30 seconds to register as a stream under Spotify's standard counting rule.

How long does a song need to play?

The 30-second threshold is a basic rule for Spotify stream counting. It does not mean that every chart, award, or third-party ranking uses the same eligibility requirements. Different charts can have their own rules and methodologies.

Do offline streams count?

Spotify allows eligible listeners to download music for offline playback. Listening while offline can be reflected in streaming data after the app reconnects to the internet. Spotify says users need to go online at least once every 30 days to keep downloaded music available.

If you listen while traveling or without a reliable connection, reconnecting to Spotify periodically allows the app to sync listening information.

Why might daily stream totals look different?

Spotify for Artists reports daily statistics using Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). Depending on where you live, the reporting day may not match your local calendar day.

Remember: A daily total shown in Spotify for Artists may cover a different time window from your local midnight-to-midnight calendar day.
02 · SUPPORT THE MUSIC

WAYS TO SUPPORT SANTOS BRAVOS

Supporting an artist can be as simple as making their music part of your everyday listening. You do not need complicated routines or special tools to explore a group's catalog and share it with other people.

Listen to official releases

Visit the official SANTOS BRAVOS artist profile and explore the available songs, singles, albums, and collaborations. You can listen to an individual track or enjoy a release in its intended order.

Save songs and albums you enjoy

Use Spotify's like or save features to keep favorite tracks and releases in your library. This makes it easier to find the music again.

Follow the artist profile

Following the official artist profile helps you keep track of the group's releases and artist activity available through Spotify.

Share official music links

Share a song, album, or artist profile with friends and other fans. When introducing someone to the group, you can include a track you think they might enjoy or a link to a full release.

Saving, following, and sharing are useful ways to engage with music. They should not be described as guaranteed ways to produce streams, playlist placements, or chart positions.
03 · PLAYLISTS

UNDERSTANDING SPOTIFY PLAYLISTS

A playlist is a collection of tracks that can be organized around an artist, mood, activity, genre, or personal preference. Spotify has several playlist types, and understanding the differences helps explain how music is organized and discovered on the platform.

Personal playlists

Personal playlists are created by Spotify listeners. You choose the songs, give the playlist a title, arrange the order, and decide whether to keep it private or share it. Fans can make playlists for favorite releases, particular moods, or a mix of artists they enjoy.

Spotify editorial playlists

Editorial playlists are curated by Spotify's editorial team. They may focus on genres, moods, new releases, or particular music scenes. Artists and their teams can submit eligible unreleased music for editorial consideration through Spotify for Artists.

Editorial consideration does not guarantee placement. Be cautious of anyone claiming they can sell guaranteed access to an official Spotify playlist.

Personalized and algorithmic playlists

Spotify also offers playlists that are personalized for individual listeners. These can reflect a person's listening history and music preferences. Examples include Discover Weekly and Release Radar.

Because these playlists are personalized, two listeners may receive different recommendations. There is no guaranteed fan-made playlist formula that forces a particular song into an algorithmic playlist.

04 · PRACTICAL WALKTHROUGH

CREATING AND ORGANIZING A PLAYLIST

Creating a playlist is a way to organize the music you want to hear. You can make one for SANTOS BRAVOS, combine different artists, or build a collection around a particular theme.

Step 1 — Create a new playlist

Open Spotify and go to Your Library. Find the option to create a playlist, then give it a title that helps you recognize its theme or purpose. You can add a description if you want to explain the playlist to other listeners.

Step 2 — Add songs

Search for a track, open its options menu, and choose the option to add it to a playlist. Select the playlist you just created. Repeat this for other songs you want to include.

Step 3 — Arrange the order

Open your playlist's editing or sorting controls to arrange tracks in the order you prefer. You might begin with a song you especially enjoy, follow it with another group release, and then include music from other artists.

Step 4 — Review and share

Play through your playlist and check that the songs appear in the order you intended. If you want other people to listen, use Spotify's sharing options to send the playlist link.

Example of a personal playlist

Here is one possible way to organize a playlist that includes SANTOS BRAVOS alongside other music:

EXAMPLE PLAYLIST ORDER

  1. SANTOS BRAVOS — Focus song
  2. Another SANTOS BRAVOS song
  3. A favorite song by another artist
  4. Another song you enjoy
  5. SANTOS BRAVOS — Another track
  6. Music from another artist

This is an example of personal playlist organization. It is not an official Spotify streaming strategy or a required sequence. You can change the order, add more tracks, or make an entirely different playlist based on your own taste.

There is no required song interval. Spotify does not publish a rule requiring fans to place a focus track at a particular position or insert another artist after a fixed number of songs.
05 · KEEP IT AUTHENTIC

LISTENING RESPONSIBLY

Spotify distinguishes genuine listening from artificial streaming. Artificial streams are plays that do not reflect authentic listening activity, including activity generated through bots, scripts, or other automated methods.

Important: Spotify prohibits artificial streaming and paid third-party services that promise streams or guaranteed playlist placement.

Practices to avoid

  • Do not use bots, scripts, or automated playback to inflate stream counts.
  • Do not repeatedly loop songs as an attempt to manipulate streaming numbers.
  • Avoid services that sell guaranteed streams, followers, or playlist placement.
  • Do not coordinate tactics intended to evade Spotify's artificial-streaming detection.
  • Do not assume that a so-called safe playlist or streaming trick is approved by Spotify.

What happens to artificial streams?

Spotify says confirmed artificial streams are not included in public stream numbers or charts. Artificial streaming can also lead to other consequences, including adjustments to reported numbers and penalties affecting artists or their music.

The best way to support SANTOS BRAVOS is to listen genuinely, discover the music, and share it with people who may enjoy it. There is no need to follow suspicious streaming instructions or purchase artificial engagement.
